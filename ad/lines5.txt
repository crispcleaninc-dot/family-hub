This book is not for perfectionists.
I tell my children all the time: I have to be able to trust you. Trust looks like this. I give you a task. I leave you to it, because I've taught you how. And when I return, it's done. Thoroughly.
That's biblical. Faithful in the little things, trusted with much. Well done, thou good and faithful servant: thou hast been faithful over a few things, I will make thee ruler over many things.
In the Bible, the word for trust means to feel safe. To rest secure in someone who is faithful. Trust in the Lord with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding.
But trust goes both ways. My children need to know me, too. So I wrote, Mommy Is Human Too.
For a postpartum mom and her newborn, trust looks different. You can't hand a baby a task. For a baby, trust is this: I cry. You come. Every feeding, every midnight, every answered cry teaches her: Mama is faithful.
Thou didst make me hope when I was upon my mother's breasts. And for you, Mama, trust is letting someone you love hold the baby while you rest. And trusting God with what you can't carry alone.
For new and postpartum moms, it's a gentle way to show your human side. Tired. Learning. Still loving.
For children, it's a way to understand the mother who labors among them.
