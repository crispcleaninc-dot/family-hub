EPA rules that apply on the call
  • Never vent. Opening the circuit = certified recovery, every time.
  • Leaks get repaired, not topped off — commercial refrigeration above the leak-rate threshold requires repair, and repeat top-offs on the same unit are the red flag.
  • Mixed/contaminated charge: recover to a separate cylinder for reclamation — never into your clean cylinder. Never mix mineral and POE oil.
  • A2L systems (R-32, R-454B, R-448A/449A blends): A2L-rated recovery machine, no ignition sources, ventilate.
  • Retrofits: R-404A → R-448A/R-449A · R-22 → R-407C/R-422B/R-438A. Oil check, seals, data-plate update — "drop-in" still means all three.

Field ID — which furnace am I looking at?
  • White PVC vent + condensate drain hose → 90%+
  • Induced draft motor, metal/B-vent, no drain → 80%
  • Open draft diverter hood, standing pilot, no inducer → 70%
  • Yellow energy tag reads the AFUE; secondary heat exchanger = 90%+ only
  • Intermittent spark: module sparks the pilot each call, sense circuit proves before the main valve — pilot faults like standing pilot, control faults like HSI
2-stage furnaces (high fire vs low fire)
  • 2-stage stat (W1 + W2 wired): big call for heat → Stage 2 immediately
  • Single-stage stat / no W2 wire: stages on time delay — Stage 2 when Stage 1 can't keep up
  • W1 = low fire, slower fans, ~½ gas · W2 = high fire, faster fans, normal gas
Key safety numbers
  • Max CO in flue gas: 400 PPM · living space: 9 PPM
  • Blower on-delay ~30 sec · off-delay ~2 min (time-based)
  • Limit normally CLOSED · pressure switch OPEN at rest
  • 90% exhaust pitch: ¼"/ft minimum, back toward the furnace

Component wiring diagrams

Condensing unit — contactor · capacitor · compressor
Reading it: Y + C from the stat energize the 24V coil; the contactor pulls in and sends L1/L2 to the load side. T1 feeds compressor C (and the fan). T2 feeds compressor R and the cap's C terminal; HERM feeds S through the capacitor, FAN feeds the condenser fan. No cooling but contactor clicks = load side. No click = coil, Y wire, or stat. Discharge the cap before touching anything.
Fan center / blower relay
How it works: G energizes the fan relay coil (FRC); the NO contact sends line voltage to the blower. Older furnaces — G contact feeds the HIGH speed tap (Y can't run the blower alone). ECM furnaces — Y runs the fan itself, so G feeds a LOW speed. The heat path bypasses this relay entirely: fan/limit switch (70%) or IFC delay (80/90) picks the heat speed.
Control transformer — 120V → 24V
Testing it: 120V across the primary and nothing across the secondary = failed transformer. Before replacing, find why — a shorted low-voltage circuit (chafed stat wire, shorted contactor coil) kills the new one too. Pull the low-voltage loads, replace, reconnect one at a time. Match the VA rating. Every 24V control in this app — stat, gas valve, contactor coil, relays — lives off this one component.
Defrost timer — commercial refrigeration
How it works: timer motor (1 → N) runs continuously. In RUN, the switch arm connects 1 → 4 and the refrigeration circuit runs. On the clock trip, the arm swaps to 1 → 2: compressor off, defrost heaters on. Terminal 3 (X) is the shortcut home — the defrost termination thermostat snaps the timer back to RUN as soon as the coil is clear, before the full time window. Diagnosing: iced coil = check initiation (timer motor turning?), heaters (ohm them), termination stat, and the drain heater. Stuck in defrost = welded contacts or failed termination.
Pump-down circuit
The sequence: box temp satisfied → t-stat opens → LLS closes → compressor keeps running, pumping the low side into the receiver → suction pressure falls → LPS opens → compressor stops. On a call: t-stat closes → LLS opens → pressure rises → LPS closes → compressor starts. The t-stat never touches the compressor — the LPS is the operating control. Short-cycling at the LPS = leaking LLS or a compressor valve letting pressure creep back.

Compressor terminal ID · ohm test

Power off, capacitor discharged, wires pulled. Highest pair = Start + Run, so the pin left out is Common; C–S (start winding) reads higher than C–R (run winding), and C–S + C–R should ≈ S–R. Any pair at 0Ω = shorted winding. Open to shell = grounded. Everything open = tripped internal overload — let it cool an hour and retest before condemning.
